# Brand Foundation

**Status:** Approved target foundation for Cyberly Public Beta 0.9
**Implementation status:** Not fully implemented

## Product Vision

Cyberly is an AI-supported cyber-wellness learning companion for Malaysian teenagers aged 13-17. It should help learners understand online risks, practise safe decisions, and feel capable of taking the next safe step.

## Product Mission

Cyberly turns cyber wellness into guided learning: learners assess what they know, study practical resources, practise scenarios, reflect on progress, and receive supportive next-step guidance.

## Brand Position

Cyberly is an AI-supported cyber wellness learning companion for Malaysian teenagers.

## Target Audience

The primary audience is Malaysian teenagers aged 13-17. The product should respect that learners are capable, curious, socially aware, and still developing judgement around online identity, privacy, scams, misinformation, and digital wellbeing.

Secondary audiences include educators, reviewers, and administrators who help maintain safe learning content. Their needs matter, but learner confidence remains the centre of the product experience.

## Malaysian Context

Cyberly should support Malaysian learners without cultural stereotyping. Use clear, inclusive English, Bahasa Melayu, and Simplified Chinese wording. Future Tamil support should not be blocked by layout or typography decisions.

Malaysia-specific safety, reporting, legal, financial, or emergency guidance must rely on reviewed official sources. The product should not invent hotline, legal, or authority claims.

## Brand Personality

- Encouraging, not hype-driven.
- Trustworthy, not cold.
- Empowering, not punitive.
- Curious, not overwhelming.
- Optimistic, not naive.
- Youthful, not childish.

Cyberly should feel like a capable digital companion with a light cyber-adventure layer. The balance is 75% Digital Companion and 25% Cyber Adventure.

## Emotional Outcomes

Learners should feel:

- "I understand this risk better."
- "I know one safe thing to do next."
- "Mistakes are part of learning."
- "Cyber safety is practical, not scary."
- "I can improve over time."

## Brand Voice

Use plain, student-friendly language. Prefer short explanations, concrete examples, and next-step guidance. Avoid shaming learners for uncertainty, wrong answers, weak progress, or unsafe assumptions.

Supportive examples:

- "Good question. Here is a safe way to think about it."
- "You do not need to master everything today. Start with one small step."
- "This is a common trick. Let us slow it down."
- "You have completed this step. A good next move is..."

Language to avoid:

- "You failed."
- "Obviously..."
- "Never make this mistake."
- "You are bad at this topic."
- "Ask anything" when the assistant is bounded to cyber wellness.

## Youthful, Not Childish

Cyberly may use friendly illustrations, soft digital accents, and warm copy. It should avoid babyish mascots, over-celebration, random jokes, and visual clutter. Teenagers should feel respected, not talked down to.

## Digital Resilience Rather Than Permanent Protection

Cyberly should not imply that learners can become permanently safe from all online harm. The goal is digital resilience: noticing risk, making safer choices, recovering from mistakes, asking for help, and improving over time.

## Core Experience Formula

Learn -> Practice -> Reflect -> Improve

This formula should shape page flows, CyberGuard guidance, action cards, progress feedback, and future learning routes.

## Core Design Principle

Make every learner feel capable of taking the next safe step.
